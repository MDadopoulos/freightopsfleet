# Freight glossary

House reference for shipment document work. One line per term, operator-grade. POD is used for two different things in this trade -- port of discharge AND proof of delivery -- so this glossary and all house copy spell out which is meant wherever the abbreviation appears.

## Documents and parties
- Bill of lading (B/L): the carrier's receipt for the cargo, the contract of carriage, and (in negotiable form) the document of title -- ocean.
- MBL / HBL: master B/L is issued by the actual carrier to the forwarder; house B/L by the forwarder to the shipper. On a master, the parties are forwarders -- expect house-level documents to name the real exporter.
- Air waybill (AWB / MAWB / HAWB): the air transport document -- non-negotiable, consignee always a named party; 11 digits with a 3-digit airline prefix. Never carries container or seal numbers.
- Sea waybill / express B/L: non-negotiable ocean transport document -- no originals to courier, cargo released to the named consignee.
- Original / negotiable B/L ("to order"): title document; an original must be surrendered for release. "TO ORDER" in the consignee box means endorsed to whoever holds it -- the notify party then matters.
- Telex release: the carrier releases cargo at destination without the physical original B/L, on the shipper's surrender at origin.
- Letter of indemnity (LOI): the shipper indemnifies the carrier for acting off-document (for example releasing without an original). Signals risk -- handle with care.
- Commercial invoice: the seller's bill to the buyer; the customs valuation basis. Line items, unit prices, totals, currency, incoterm.
- Proforma invoice: pre-shipment, quote-shaped invoice -- not valid for a customs entry.
- Packing list: per-package contents, weights, and dimensions -- the detail authority for counts and weights; carries no prices.
- Certificate of origin (COO): attests country of origin; needed for preferential (FTA) duty claims; often chamber-stamped -- the slowest document to obtain.
- Shipper's letter of instruction (SLI): the shipper's booking and documentation brief to the forwarder.
- Manifest: the carrier's full cargo declaration filed with customs for the voyage or flight.
- Arrival notice: the carrier or agent's advice that cargo is arriving, with charges due.
- Delivery order (D/O): the release instruction to the terminal or carrier once charges are settled.
- POD (port of discharge): where cargo leaves the vessel. POD (proof of delivery): the signed delivery receipt. Same letters, different documents -- always say which.
- EIR (equipment interchange receipt): records container condition and seal at every gate move -- the reference for seal and damage disputes.
- Shipper / consignee / notify party: seller-exporter / receiver / who gets the arrival notice. The notify party should be the buyer or their customs broker.
- Freight forwarder: arranges transport on other people's assets; issues house documents.
- NVOCC: a forwarder that issues its own house B/Ls and buys vessel space wholesale -- no vessels of its own.
- Customs broker: licensed to file customs entries; owns HS-code and valuation liability -- code questions go to the broker.
- Carrier (VOCC / airline): actually moves the cargo and issues the master transport document.

## Operations and equipment
- FCL / LCL: full container load / less-than-container-load (shared container, charged per W/M).
- FTL / LTL: the trucking equivalents.
- CY / CFS: container yard (FCL handoff point) / container freight station (where LCL is stuffed and stripped).
- TEU / FEU: twenty- / forty-foot equivalent unit -- container count units.
- Demurrage: the charge for a container sitting INSIDE the terminal past free time. Clock runs per day, tariffs escalate.
- Detention (per diem): the charge for holding the container OUTSIDE the terminal past free time.
- Free time: the included days before demurrage or detention start -- typically 4-7; fewer at congested ports. Where the money hides in a cheap quote.
- Drayage: the port-to-warehouse truck leg.
- Chassis: the trailer frame a container rides on -- a separate, mostly US, fee.
- Cut-off (doc cut / CY cut): the deadlines for documents and for gate-in before the sailing. Miss them and the booking rolls.
- Rollover: booked cargo bumped to a later vessel -- no fault implied, but the clock and the customer both care.
- Transshipment: cargo changes vessel at an intermediate port -- cheaper, slower, one more place to misconnect.
- VGM: verified gross mass -- the SOLAS-mandated certified container weight before loading.
- Reefer: temperature-controlled container (ISO type codes with R, for example 45R1).
- SOC / COC: shipper-owned vs carrier-owned container.
- ULD: air unit load device -- the aircraft pallets and containers.
- General average: a declared maritime loss shared across all cargo interests -- rare and catastrophic; insurance territory.
- ISO 6346 container number: 4 letters (owner + category, usually ending U) + 6 digits + 1 check digit. A number whose check digit fails is a typo by definition.
- Voyage number: the sailing's identifier, usually with an E/W (eastbound/westbound) suffix that should match the lane's direction.

## Charges and pricing
- THC: terminal handling charge -- at BOTH ends; a quote excluding destination THC is not all-in.
- BAF / CAF: bunker (fuel) / currency adjustment surcharges.
- GRI: general rate increase -- carriers raising spot rates, often mid-quote-validity via subject-to clauses.
- PSS: peak season surcharge.
- W/M (revenue ton): LCL charge basis -- max(1000 kg, 1 CBM).
- Chargeable weight (air): max(gross weight, volumetric weight); volumetric = length x width x height in cm / 6000.
- Spot vs contract rate: the market rate of the day vs a term agreement; spot quotes carry validity windows.
- All-in rate: allegedly everything included -- verify both ends' charges before believing it.
- Freight prepaid / collect: who pays the carrier, at which end -- must cohere with the incoterm (C- and D-terms prepaid; E- and F-terms collect).

## Customs and compliance
- HS code: the harmonized tariff code; first 6 digits are world-common, national codes extend to 8-10. Duty and admissibility hang on it; the customs broker owns it.
- Customs entry / clearance: the import declaration and its release process; blocked without the document set.
- Customs hold / exam: cargo stopped for inspection -- document fixes are the operator's to make; exam timing is not.
- ISF (10+2): the US importer security filing, due before vessel loading at origin.
- AMS / ENS: the US / EU advance manifest filings.
- Bonded warehouse: duty-suspended storage under customs control.
- DG / IMDG class: dangerous goods and their ocean classification -- must be declared, packed, and certified; an undeclared battery shipment is a stop-everything problem.

## Incoterms 2020 (all eleven)
Where risk transfers and who arranges freight. E/F-terms: buyer arranges main carriage (freight COLLECT); C/D-terms: seller arranges (freight PREPAID).
- EXW Ex Works: buyer collects at the seller's door; maximum buyer burden.
- FCA Free Carrier: seller hands to the buyer's carrier at a named point -- the technically correct container term, though operators still say FOB.
- FAS Free Alongside Ship: seller delivers alongside the vessel.
- FOB Free On Board: risk passes when goods are on board; buyer books and pays the main leg. The default China-export term in practice.
- CFR Cost and Freight: seller pays ocean freight to destination; risk still passes at loading.
- CIF Cost, Insurance and Freight: CFR plus seller-provided insurance.
- CPT / CIP Carriage (and Insurance) Paid To: the multimodal cousins of CFR/CIF.
- DAP Delivered At Place: seller delivers to the named place, buyer clears import.
- DPU Delivered at Place Unloaded: DAP plus unloading.
- DDP Delivered Duty Paid: seller does everything including import duty -- maximum seller burden.
